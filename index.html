Every edition stands on the stories printed before it.

FOLIO is a game about the press — and about how a story earns its place by what it builds upon. That idea isn't ours. We learned it from the Obyte community, whose work on a directed acyclic graph — a web where each new record cites the ones beneath it, with no central press deciding what's true — taught us to see attribution as a living structure rather than a straight line.

When we were finding our footing, Obyte helped us out. This page is our thank-you, set in the oldest way a paper knows how to say it: in the colophon, where the printer signs the work.

Folio: the ball is the day's story. Don't get scooped.
You are an Editor. Hire papers from the classifieds — three identical hires merge into a ★ veteran masthead — and set up to LEVEL of them in the columns of your half-page. In combat the newsroom coordinates itself: one paper takes the story, the rest cover lanes, and they drop passes back to set up harder returns — while your mouse steers the goalie on the back line (SPACE toggles auto-goalie). Let the story past, and the rival goes to press: you bleed readership. At zero, your paper folds. Each rally has a 60-second clock; the last 15 are OVERTIME, when the story runs scalding. Between days, choose Headlines and buy goalie power-ups. Eight rivals stand on the ladder, from a cub reporter to the machine itself. Everything you print files onto THE WIRE SERVICE — each story cites fresh ones still on the wire (same desk, same price). A well-sourced hire earns the front page (next hire −1¢), and deep chains grow circulation: +1¢ a morning per 4 stories of depth. Rival goals file dead ends in red ink that crowd your stories off the wire.
